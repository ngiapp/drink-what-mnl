Almost every drink here starts as the same plant. What separates a grassy green from a malty black is oxidation — how long the picked leaf reacts with air before heat stops it. Knowing roughly where a tea sits on that line tells you how it will taste before you order.

0%Green 10–30%Light oolong 40–70%Roasted oolong 70–80%Red oolong 100%Black

Two sit outside the line: matcha is whole leaf ground to powder, and herbal drinks contain no tea plant at all — which is where wintermelon lives, and why it has no caffeine. One thing worth clearing up: the Big 3 that Filipino menus are built on — Hokkaido, Okinawa and Wintermelon — are flavour styles, not tea types. Hokkaido and Okinawa are both black tea underneath, dressed with butter-toffee and brown sugar respectively. Wintermelon is not tea at all. And sinkers, the local word for what goes in the bottom, are a separate choice from the tea itself.

The nine families, and when to pick each

How the prices work. Philippine chains do not publish per-drink prices on their own sites, and delivery apps mark theirs up. So every price declares where it came from, visible when you open a drink: sourced prices (₱138) come from a dated 2026 menu listing; estimates carry a tilde (~₱140) and sit inside that shop’s price band. 30 of the 67 prices here are sourced figures. Sizes, branches, sinkers and promos all move the real number.

This edition is a second pass, not a sixth. The Singapore edition was rebuilt repeatedly as errors surfaced — two chains that had already left the country, a price wrong by a third, drinks that did not exist. This build has had one such correction: CHAGEE and CHICHA San Chen were missing entirely, despite CHAGEE having opened here on 29 August 2025 and grown to 21 Metro Manila stores, and CHICHA having traded in Makati since February 2023. Both were absent for the same bad reason — they did not appear in the Philippine brand roundups this edition was first built from, and absence from a listicle was mistaken for absence from the market.

Those two are now the best-sourced shops here, because both publish a real Philippine site. Every CHICHA drink name comes from chichasanchen.ph, and its pricing is arithmetic rather than estimate: a tea base is ₱125, a flavouring adds ₱45, a topping adds ₱25. CHAGEE’s own store directory confirms 21 branches and its six series. The remaining nine chains still rest on dated menu listings, and four of them — Happy Lemon, Tiger Sugar, Yi Fang, Sharetea — have no itemised prices found at all and carry a warning when you open their drinks.

Gong Cha is alive and everywhere here — worth stating because it closed all 29 of its Singapore outlets in October 2025 and still has not relaunched there. The same brand, two very different markets.

Same brand, different menu. CHICHA San Chen runs a Taro Series here — taro balls, taro paste, taro bubble — that has no equivalent on its Singapore board, where the same chain sells pure tea and mousse instead. Worth remembering before assuming a regional chain carries the same drinks everywhere.

No Nutri-Grade. The A–D letter grades on Singapore drink menus come from a Health Promotion Board scheme that has no Philippine equivalent, so this edition shows no grade rather than inventing one. Sugar is quoted the way counters here actually ask: 0, 25, 50, 75 or 100 per cent.

The cups are drawn, not photographed. Every illustration is generated from that drink’s own record — the tea base sets the colour, milk lightens it, foam and sinkers and brown-sugar striping come off the topping list.
